# Groww SIP Continuity — Form-Based Qualitative Research Notes

**Collection method:** Asynchronous Google Form questionnaire (not live or moderated interviews)  
**Collection dates:** 28–29 October 2026 for the six included responses  
**Sample:** Six respondents who answered **Yes** to having started their first mutual fund SIP. One additional response from 31 October is excluded from the primary analysis because it answered **No** to having started a SIP while also reporting a paused instalment.  
**Evidence:** Original Google Form response table provided by the project author. All participant summaries below are paraphrases of recorded responses, not fabricated verbatim interview quotations. Questions were mostly structured, so these data should not be represented as live, probing interviews.

## Cross-participant synthesis

| Finding | Observed in included responses | Product implication (hypothesis, not measured effect) |
|---|---|---|
| Payment continuity varies | 3 running as planned; 2 running with a skipped/paused instalment; 1 stopped/cancelled | Distinguish active, interrupted and stopped states rather than assuming every investor needs recovery |
| External information seeking | 4/6 search Google or YouTube; 2/6 read Reddit/forums when unsure | Offer clear explanations within the experience while preserving access to independent information |
| Competing expenses | P04 needed money elsewhere; P05 cited monthly expenses/salary timing | Explain choices without guilt, pressure, or investment recommendations |
| Performance uncertainty | P02 cited confusion about performance; P01 requested simple explanations | Explain uncertainty plainly without predicting returns |
| Desired assistance | 2/6 reminders; 1/6 performance explanations; 1/6 pause/resume; 1/6 payment failures; 1/6 nothing | Provide contextual, optional support instead of one generic intervention |
| Willingness to test later | 5 Yes, 1 Maybe | Possible recruitment leads, **not** evidence that usability testing occurred |

## Research interpretation and design recommendations

1. **Prioritize clear payment and SIP-state explanations.** A user's stated status and the reason for interruption should be clarified rather than inferred from a generic complaint.
2. **Offer neutral, user-controlled next steps.** Explain how to check payment status or find pause/resume information, without encouraging unsuitable contributions.
3. **Support uncertainty without financial recommendations.** Plain-language education can address questions about performance; do not recommend particular funds or guarantee outcomes.
4. **Avoid unnecessary nudges.** P03 explicitly wanted no additional support, suggesting optionality matters.
5. **Test the prototype separately.** The form asked whether participants *would* test later; it did not record actual prototype tasks, completion, usability issues, or test outcomes.

## Method and limitations

- This is a **six-participant form-based qualitative research synthesis**, not evidence of six synchronous interviews.
- Responses are mainly multiple-choice selections. No follow-up probing, interview duration, or direct quotes beyond the selected response text are documented.
- The sample is small, self-selected, and spans multiple investment platforms; it cannot establish representative Groww user prevalence.
- Respondents' exact first-year eligibility, transactions, and month-12 active SIP outcomes cannot be verified from these answers alone.
- The excluded seventh row contains internally inconsistent eligibility/status answers and should be reviewed before inclusion.
- No independent usability sessions are documented in this source.
